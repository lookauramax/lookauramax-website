Free Calorie Calculator (TDEE)

By Lookauramax Team · Published 17 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

Your TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure — is the number of calories your body uses in a typical day. This calculator estimates it from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation plus your activity level, then shows a maintenance range. Enter your details below to get your number.

What is the Calorie Calculator?

The Calorie Calculator is a free tool that estimates your daily energy needs. It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation with your age, height, weight, and activity level. It gives a TDEE estimate for general planning. It is not a prescription.

How to use this tool

  1. Enter your age, height and weight.
  2. Choose your sex and activity level.
  3. Select Calculate my calories to see your TDEE.
  4. Read your maintenance range and the guidance beside it.

How to use your number

Your TDEE is a starting point, not a prescription. Track your weight and energy for 2–4 weeks eating near maintenance: if your weight is steady, the estimate is close. If it drifts, adjust by 100–200 kcal at a time. The most reliable "diet" is the one you can actually repeat — protein at each meal, vegetables, water, sleep, and daily movement beat any extreme plan that lasts a week.

Frequently asked questions

What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is an estimate of the calories you burn in a day, including core body functions plus activity. Eating around your TDEE roughly maintains your weight; eating below it tends to reduce weight over time, and eating above it tends to increase it.

How accurate is this calorie calculator?

It is an estimate based on the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is the most widely validated formula for resting metabolism. Real needs vary with body composition, hormones, medication, and daily activity. Treat the result as a starting point and adjust based on real-world results over 2 to 4 weeks.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight safely?

A commonly suggested approach is a modest deficit of roughly 300–500 kcal per day below maintenance, combined with protein and strength training. Very low intakes — below about 1,200 kcal per day for women or 1,500 for men — need professional supervision and are not recommended on your own.
