How to actually hit it

  • Start the day with a glass — overnight you lose fluid through breathing and sweat.
  • Keep water visible — a bottle on your desk beats willpower.
  • Drink with meals — it is the easiest habit anchor there is.
  • Check the color test: pale straw urine usually means you are hydrated; dark yellow means drink more. Clear-as-water every time can mean you are overdoing it.
  • Exercise days: drink during and after training, and replace what you sweated out — do not wait until you are very thirsty.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I drink per day?

A common general guide is roughly 30–35 ml per kilogram of body weight per day, plus extra for exercise and heat. For a 70 kg adult, that is about 2.1–2.5 litres. Needs vary widely with climate, activity, health conditions, and medication.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes. Drinking far more water than your body needs can dilute blood sodium (hyponatremia), which is dangerous. Do not force extreme volumes — spread intake through the day and let thirst and urine color guide you.

Does coffee or tea count as water?

Mostly yes. Coffee, tea, and other unsweetened drinks hydrate you, and food provides roughly a fifth of daily water. Alcohol does not count — it increases fluid loss.
