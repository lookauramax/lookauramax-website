Glow-Up Glossary: Looksmaxing Terms Explained Honestly

By Lookauramax Team · Published 17 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

The glow-up world is full of jargon — some useful, some invented to sell you things. This glossary defines 25 of the most common terms in plain language, including an honest verdict on the claims that do not hold up.

Core concepts

Looksmaxing
Working to improve your appearance through grooming, skincare, fitness, style, posture, and habits. The honest version focuses on things you can actually change — not on rating your bone structure or chasing an impossible face shape. See our looksmaxing guide for the full picture.
Auramaxing
Building your overall presence — grooming, confidence, posture, and how you carry yourself — rather than chasing a single facial feature. It is the idea behind Lookauramax: your "aura" is the whole impression you make, not one number.
Glow-up
A visible improvement in how you look and carry yourself over time, usually from consistent grooming, health, style, and confidence habits. A real glow-up takes weeks and months, not a weekend. Our 90-day glow-up guide lays out a realistic version.
Confidence
Trust in your own ability to handle situations, built from repeated experience and kept promises to yourself — not a fixed personality trait. It is situational: you can be confident in one setting and nervous in another. See our confidence guide.
First impression
The quick judgement people form in the first seconds of meeting you, influenced by grooming, posture, expression, and behaviour. First impressions are real but not permanent — they update as people get to know you.
Body language
Non-verbal signals — posture, gestures, eye contact, facial expression — that shape how others read you. Small changes, like standing tall and making comfortable eye contact, change how you come across more than any "trick" does.
Posture
How you hold your body while standing, sitting, and moving. Good posture affects comfort, presence, and how you appear in photos. See our posture guide for simple daily habits.

Face terms — and what the evidence says

Mewing
Pressing the tongue to the roof of the mouth, promoted online as a way to reshape the jaw. The honest verdict: there is no reliable evidence that mewing changes jawline or bone structure in adults. Breathing through your nose and keeping good tongue posture are healthy habits, but they are not a jawline treatment. Our jawline truth guide explains what actually affects jawline appearance.
Face yoga
Facial exercises promoted for slimming or lifting the face. There is no reliable evidence that face yoga burns face fat or reshapes bone. Gentle facial movement is harmless, but it is not a fat-loss method.
Spot reduction
The idea that you can burn fat from one specific body area by exercising it — for example, that chewing exercises burn face fat. This is a myth. Fat loss happens across the whole body through overall nutrition and activity, not from targeted exercises.
Jawline
The visible lower edge of the jaw. Its appearance depends on body fat, genetics, grooming, posture, lighting, and photo angle — not on exercises that claim to burn face fat. Beard shaping and posture can change how defined it looks.
Cheekbones
The bony structure under the eyes. Their prominence is largely genetic; lighting, grooming, and overall body composition affect how visible they look. No exercise changes their shape.
Hunter eyes
Internet slang for deep-set, intense-looking eyes. A subjective aesthetic label, not a medical or measurable trait. It is not something you can — or should try to — train.
Canthal tilt
The angle of the line between the inner and outer corners of the eye. An internet-obsessed measurement with no meaningful use for self-improvement. Ignore it.

Body & nutrition terms

BMI
Body Mass Index: weight divided by height squared. A general screening number, not a diagnosis. It does not account for muscle, bone density, or body composition, so athletic people can score "overweight" while being perfectly healthy.
BMR
Basal Metabolic Rate: the energy your body uses at rest to keep you alive. It depends on age, sex, height, and weight, and is estimated by formulas — it is not a precise personal measurement.
TDEE
Total Daily Energy Expenditure: the total energy your body uses in a day, including activity. TDEE = BMR × an activity multiplier. It is an estimate used for general nutrition planning, not a prescription.
Protein
A macronutrient your body uses to build and repair tissue. General guides suggest roughly 0.8 g per kg of body weight for sedentary adults and more for active people, but personal needs vary. General information, not a prescription.

Skin & hair terms

Retinol
A vitamin A derivative used in skincare, widely discussed for skin texture and ageing. General information only — not medical advice. It can irritate sensitive skin, and people with skin conditions, or who are pregnant, should consult a clinician before use.
Sunscreen / SPF
A product that protects skin from UV rays. SPF indicates protection against UVB rays; "broad spectrum" covers UVA too. Daily sunscreen use is widely recommended by dermatologists and is one of the simplest long-term skin habits.
Skin barrier
The outer layer of skin that keeps moisture in and irritants out. Over-exfoliating, harsh products, or too many actives at once can damage it, causing redness, dryness, and breakouts.
Double cleansing
Washing the face twice: first with an oil-based or micellar cleanser, then with a water-based cleanser. A technique some people find helpful for removing sunscreen and makeup — not a requirement for everyone.
Hair porosity
How well hair absorbs and holds moisture. Low-porosity hair resists moisture; high-porosity hair absorbs fast but dries fast. It affects which products and routines suit your hair. See our hair grooming guide.
Split ends
Hair ends that have frayed or split from damage. They cannot be repaired by any product; trimming is the standard fix. Conditioning can reduce future splitting.
Beard neckline
The shaped lower edge of a beard, usually just above the Adam's apple. A clean neckline makes a beard look deliberate rather than unkempt. See our beard growth guide.

Frequently asked questions

What does looksmaxing mean?

Looksmaxing means working to improve your appearance through grooming, skincare, fitness, style, posture, and habits. The honest version focuses on things you can actually change — not on rating your bone structure or chasing an impossible face shape.

Does mewing actually change your jawline?

There is no reliable evidence that mewing — pressing your tongue to the roof of your mouth — changes jawline or bone structure in adults. Good tongue posture and breathing through your nose are healthy habits, but they are not a jawline treatment.

Is spot reduction real?

No. Spot reduction — the idea that you can burn fat from one specific body area by exercising it — is a myth. Fat loss happens across the whole body through overall nutrition and activity, not from targeted exercises.

What is the difference between BMI, BMR, and TDEE?

BMI is a height-and-weight ratio used as a general screening number. BMR is the energy your body uses at rest. TDEE is your total daily energy use including activity. All three are estimates and general information, not medical diagnoses.
