Movember: The Moustache-Growing Guide

Movember 2026 runs from Sunday, November 1 to Monday, November 30. You start clean-shaven, grow a moustache for the month, and use it to start conversations about men's health. Here is how to take part properly — and end the month with a moustache that looks deliberate.

What is Movember?

Movember is an annual November campaign run by the Movember Foundation. Participants grow a moustache through the month to raise funds and start conversations about three areas of men's health: prostate cancer, testicular cancer, and mental health and suicide prevention. The moustache is the symbol — Movember describes it as a conversation starter, and the campaign's founding idea is that a visible change on your face makes people ask why, which gives you the opening to talk about health men usually avoid.

The scale of the problem is the point. Movember notes that men die on average years earlier than women, that prostate cancer rates are projected to climb sharply, and that around three quarters of suicides are men. The moustache is lighthearted. The reason for it is not.

The official rules

  1. Start clean-shaven on November 1. Everyone begins from a bare face — no head starts. Growing early goes against the spirit of the campaign.
  2. Grow and groom your moustache for the entire month. Thirty days, November 1 to November 30.
  3. No faking it. Under the official rules the moustache stands alone: no beard, no goatee, and no fake moustaches.
  4. Use it. The moustache exists to create conversation and raise funds for men's health. When people ask, tell them why you are growing it.

Cannot grow one, or would rather move? Movember offers other ways in: Move — walk or run 60 kilometres across November, a number chosen for the 60 men lost to suicide every hour around the world; Host a Mo-ment — a gathering that raises money; or Mo Your Own Way — a challenge of your own design. Sign-up and fundraising run through movember.com.

One common confusion, settled: Movember is not No-Shave November. No-Shave November is a separate campaign in which participants skip shaving entirely and donate the saved grooming money to a cancer charity of their choice. Movember is moustache-only, run by one foundation, for men's health causes specifically.

Week by week: growing a moustache that works

Week 1 — the sparse start

Shave everything on November 1 and take a photo; you will want the comparison later. Days three to seven look like you forgot to shave rather than chose not to — every participant goes through this, and it is the week most men quit. Keep the rest of your face clean-shaven daily so the upper lip reads as a decision. Wash your face as usual and moisturise; the skin above your lip is adjusting to being covered.

Week 2 — shape appears

Growth starts to read as a moustache. Resist trimming the body of it — length is what makes later styling possible. You may clean the line directly above your lip so hairs do not catch on food and drink, using small scissors and a steady hand. If the skin beneath itches, moisturise the skin itself, not only the hair.

Week 3 — train the direction

Comb the moustache daily in the direction you want it to lie — down and slightly out from the centre for a classic shape. A small amount of beard balm gives light hold and keeps strays in line. This is the week the moustache starts to look like the finished thing, and the week conversations start, which is the campaign working as designed.

Week 4 — choose the finish

Decide your style (below) and tidy the edges. Many men book a barber for the final days of the month to set the shape cleanly — a good moment to decide whether the moustache stays into December or ends with the campaign. Take the end-of-month photo either way.

Choosing a style your growth can support

  • The chevron. Full, straight across, covering the top of the lip. The classic Movember shape; needs moderate-to-strong growth and regular tidying of the lip line.
  • The painter's brush. Neater and uniform in width, sitting above the lip. Forgiving for average growth because tidiness does the work.
  • The handlebar. Longer ends trained outward and slightly up. Needs length and patience — realistic mainly for men whose growth is fast and thick, or as a December project.
  • The short, kept moustache. For slower or finer growth: keep it shorter, edges exact, skin well cared for. A shorter moustache with clean edges looks deliberate; a wispy one left untended does not.

The honest rule: density is mostly genetic, and no oil changes your follicles. Work with the growth you have — our beard growth guide explains honestly what can and cannot be influenced, and the advice transfers to the moustache.

Care for the skin under a growing moustache

  • Wash daily. A gentle cleanser over the whole face, worked through the moustache to the skin beneath, keeps oil and flakes from building up.
  • Moisturise the skin, not only the hair. Part the hair with your fingers and get moisturiser down to the skin. Dry, flaky skin under a moustache is the most common reason a good one looks scruffy.
  • Add a few drops of oil once there is length. Once the hair covers the skin, a small amount of beard oil softens the hair and reduces itch. A little is enough.
  • Exfoliate gently once or twice a week. This lifts flakes and helps prevent ingrown hairs around the edges — gently, and never on irritated skin.

The health part is the main part

A moustache that raises no conversation and no money is a style experiment. Three things give the month its meaning:

  • Know your own basics. Movember encourages men to know their numbers and family history, to check their testicles regularly and see a doctor promptly about any lump or change, and to talk to a doctor about prostate health at the age their national guidance suggests — earlier with a family history.
  • Check in on your friends properly. Movember's mental-health work centres on conversation: ask, listen without rushing to fix, encourage professional support where it is needed. International Men's Day falls on November 19, inside the campaign.
  • Say why you are growing it. Every "what is that on your face?" is the campaign working. Have your one sentence ready.

If anything about your own health worries you — a lump, a change, a low mood that will not lift — the correct move is a doctor or a qualified professional, this month rather than someday.

Frequently asked questions

What are the official Movember rules?

Start clean-shaven on November 1, grow and groom a moustache for the whole month, and keep it a moustache only — no beard, no goatee, nothing fake. The point of the rules is the conversation: use the moustache to talk about men's health and raise funds.

Can I grow a beard for Movember instead?

Under the official rules, no — Movember is moustache-only, and the rest of the face stays clean-shaven so the moustache stands out. If you want to grow everything, that is No-Shave November, a separate campaign supporting cancer causes.

How long does a moustache take to look full?

Most men need three to four weeks before a moustache reads as intentional rather than accidental. Growth speed and density are mostly genetic, so judge yours at the end of the month, not in week one.

What if my moustache grows in patchy?

Keep it shorter and the edges clean — a shorter, tidy moustache looks deliberate even with modest density. Moisturise the skin beneath, avoid trimming the body of it early, and let the full month pass before deciding it does not suit you.
