Oily Skin: A Clear Routine That Works

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

Oily skin is not a flaw to be scrubbed away. It is a skin type that needs the right textures, a gentle routine, and one big mindset shift: less force, more consistency.

What is oily skin?

Oily skin means skin that produces more sebum than average. It can look shiny and feel greasy by midday. It is a normal skin type, not a flaw. Gentle cleansing, light moisturiser, and sunscreen suit it. Harsh stripping makes it worse.

If your face is shiny by midday, your pores look large, and breakouts cluster on your forehead, nose, and chin, you probably have oily skin — and you have probably been told to fight it with stronger washes, more products, and oil-free everything. That advice usually backfires. The most common reason oily skin stays oily is over-treatment: harsh cleansing strips the barrier, skin panics, and oil production rises to compensate. The routine that works is gentler than you expect, and it starts with accepting that some oil is healthy. Skin oil (sebum) protects the barrier and keeps skin supple. The goal is balanced, comfortable skin — not a matte, tight face.

Why your skin is oily

Oil production is driven mainly by genetics and hormones, with climate, stress, and dehydration playing supporting roles. Hot, humid weather increases shine. Stress raises cortisol, which can increase oil. Dehydrated skin — including oily skin that has been over-washed — overproduces oil to compensate. You cannot change your genetics, but you can change the conditions: a gentle routine, the right textures, hydration, and sun protection make a real, visible difference within a few weeks.

One useful reframe: oily skin ages more slowly than dry skin in one respect — the extra oil keeps the barrier supple and fine lines often show later. Oily skin is not a punishment; it is a skin type with its own advantages and its own clear maintenance.

The oily-skin routine

Morning

  1. Gentle cleanser. A gel or foaming cleanser, lukewarm water, 30–60 seconds. Fragrance-free. One wash — not two, not a scrub.
  2. Light moisturiser. A gel or light lotion, oil-free, non-comedogenic. Apply to slightly damp skin. Yes, even oily skin needs moisturiser — skipping it is the most common oily-skin mistake.
  3. Sunscreen. Broad-spectrum SPF 30+, in a gel or light fluid texture. Gel sunscreens sit best on oily skin. Apply the full amount; a thin layer does not give the labelled protection.

Night

  1. Cleanser. Wash off sunscreen, sweat, and the day. If you wear heavy sunscreen or makeup, a micellar or oil first step followed by your gel cleanser helps.
  2. Light moisturiser. Same gel or lotion. If your skin feels tight after cleansing, your cleanser is too harsh — switch to a gentler one, do not add a heavier cream.

Optional: one active, 2–3 nights a week

Salicylic acid (a BHA, 0.5–2%) is the best-studied active for oily, clog-prone skin because it dissolves inside pores. Use it two or three nights a week after cleansing, before moisturiser. Niacinamide (2–5%) can help with oil and uneven tone. Introduce one at a time, wait two weeks before judging, and stop anything that causes persistent burning or peeling. Never stack two strong actives on the same night.

Managing midday shine

  • Blot, do not wash. Blotting papers or a single tissue pressed gently on the T-zone remove oil without stripping the barrier. Washing midday usually makes shine return faster.
  • One light powder, if you like it. A single dusting of translucent powder on the T-zone is fine. Layering powder all day cakes, clogs, and looks worse.
  • Keep hands off. Touching spreads oil and bacteria. Hands off your face is a genuine oily-skin strategy.
  • Check your hair products. Heavy pomades, oils, and waxes migrate onto the forehead and clog pores. Keep styling products off the face and wash the hairline in your evening cleanse.

What makes oily skin worse

  • Over-washing and harsh scrubs. Washing more than twice a day, hot water, and gritty scrubs strip the barrier and increase oil and breakouts.
  • Alcohol-heavy toners and astringents. The tight, squeaky feeling is barrier damage, not cleanliness. Skip them.
  • Skipping moisturiser. Dehydrated oily skin overproduces oil. A light gel moisturiser is your friend, not your enemy.
  • Heavy, pore-clogging products. Thick creams, heavy oils, and comedogenic formulas clog oily pores. Choose gel, lotion, and "non-comedogenic" labels.
  • Adding five actives at once. Retinol plus acids plus benzoyl peroxide plus scrubs is a recipe for irritation. One active at a time.
  • Skipping sunscreen because it feels greasy. The fix is a gel sunscreen you like, not skipping the step. Sun damage worsens dark marks and ageing for every skin type.

When oily skin needs a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if oil comes with persistent, painful, or scarring breakouts; if your skin is suddenly much oilier with other changes (such as hair changes or irregular cycles — worth discussing with a doctor, as hormones may be involved); or if no routine has helped after 8–12 weeks. A dermatologist can discuss prescription options and rule out conditions that mimic oily skin. Most oily skin, though, responds well to the clear routine above — given a fair month to work.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best routine for oily skin?

Gentle cleanser twice a day, a light gel or lotion moisturiser, and daily sunscreen. Add one active such as salicylic acid two or three nights a week if pores clog. That is the complete routine.

Does oily skin need moisturiser?

Yes. Skipping moisturiser dehydrates skin, which can make it overproduce oil. A light, oil-free, non-comedogenic gel or lotion moisturiser suits oily skin.

How do I stop my face getting shiny by midday?

Blotting papers or a single tissue pressed on the T-zone remove oil without stripping skin. Avoid washing your face midday or piling on powder, which usually makes shine return faster.

Does washing more reduce oily skin?

No. Washing more than twice a day strips the skin barrier and can increase oil production and breakouts. Twice a day with a gentle cleanser is enough.
