How to Wash Your Face the Right Way

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

Face washing is the most core skincare step — and the one most people get wrong. The right way takes 60 seconds, costs almost nothing, and protects every other product you use.

What is face washing?

Face washing means cleaning your skin with water and a gentle cleanser. It removes oil, sweat, sunscreen, and dirt. It should leave skin comfortable, not tight or dry. Twice a day is enough for most people. Harsh scrubbing does more harm than good.

Washing your face seems too clear to need a guide. Yet walk through any skincare aisle and you will find harsh scrubs, "deep clean" foams that leave skin squeaky, and tools that promise to blast your pores clean. The result is a generation of people who wash their faces aggressively, strip their skin barrier, and then wonder why their skin is dry, red, and breaking out. Face washing is not about maximum cleaning power. It is about removing dirt, oil, sunscreen, and sweat while leaving your skin barrier intact. Done right, it is boring. Boring is the point.

This guide covers the exact method, the common mistakes, and how to pick a cleanser without overthinking it. It applies to every skin type, with small notes where oily, dry, or sensitive skin differs.

The right way, step by step

  1. Clean hands first. Wash your hands before touching your face. Hands carry bacteria, oil, and whatever you touched last. Skipping this step smears it onto your face.
  2. Lukewarm water. Splash your face with lukewarm water — never hot, never ice cold. Hot water strips oils and worsens redness; lukewarm water cleans just as well.
  3. A small amount of gentle cleanser. A pea-sized to coin-sized amount is enough. Lather it between your palms, then massage it over your face with your fingertips.
  4. 60 seconds, gentle circles. Spend about a minute, covering forehead, nose, chin, cheeks, jawline, and hairline. Use light fingertip pressure — no scrubbing, no nails, no washcloth friction. Pay attention to the nose creases and jawline, where buildup hides.
  5. Rinse thoroughly. Splash 10–15 times until no cleanser remains. Leftover cleanser dries skin out and can irritate it. Rinse the hairline and jawline carefully — residue there causes breakouts along the edges of the face.
  6. Pat dry with a clean towel. Pat, don't rub. Rubbing with a towel irritates skin and spreads bacteria if the towel is dirty. Use a clean towel or a fresh face cloth, and change it every few days.
  7. Moisturise while slightly damp. Apply moisturiser within a minute of washing, while skin is still a little damp. Damp skin absorbs moisturiser better and stays hydrated longer.

Total time: about ninety seconds, including moisturiser. If your face-washing routine takes longer than that, it is probably doing more harm than good.

How often, and when

Twice a day — morning and night — is right for most people. Morning removes oil and sweat built up while you sleep. Night removes sunscreen, pollution, and the day. Add a third wash after heavy sweating or a workout, and rinse with water only if your skin feels tight after three full washes. Very dry or sensitive skin can sometimes manage with one full cleanse at night and a water rinse in the morning; oily skin usually needs both. Listen to your skin: tight, shiny-in-a-bad-way, or stinging skin after washing means you are overdoing it.

Choosing a cleanser (without overthinking)

A good cleanser is gentle, fragrance-free, and leaves your skin feeling comfortable — not squeaky, not tight. Gel or foaming cleansers suit oily and combination skin. Cream or lotion cleansers suit dry and sensitive skin. Micellar water works as a backup for travel or a quick rinse, but it is not a replacement for a proper wash at home. Price is a poor guide: many inexpensive cleansers are excellent, and many expensive ones are harsh. Read the feel, not the marketing: if your skin feels stripped after washing, the cleanser is too strong for you, whatever the price.

What a cleanser does not need to do: exfoliate, "detox," tingle, foam dramatically, or smell like a spa. A cleanser sits on your skin for sixty seconds and then goes down the drain. Spend your money on moisturiser and sunscreen, not on a luxury wash.

Common mistakes that cause breakouts

  • Hot water. Strips the barrier, increases redness, and makes skin produce more oil to compensate.
  • Scrubbing and tools. Harsh scrubs, rough brushes, and washcloth friction irritate skin and spread bacteria. Fingertips are the best tool.
  • Over-washing. Washing three or four times a day dries skin out, and dry, irritated skin breaks out more — not less.
  • Skipping the rinse. Cleanser residue along the hairline and jawline causes edge breakouts that people blame on hormones or diet.
  • Using body soap or shampoo on your face. Body products are formulated for thicker skin and are usually too harsh for the face. Use a face cleanser.
  • Rubbing dry with a dirty towel. A damp towel left for days grows bacteria. Pat dry with a clean one.
  • Skipping moisturiser. Washing without moisturising leaves skin dehydrated, and dehydrated skin overproduces oil. Even oily skin needs moisturiser.
  • DIY "cleansers." Lemon juice, baking soda, toothpaste, and apple cider vinegar are not skincare. They burn, irritate, and disrupt skin pH. Keep them in the kitchen.

When washing is not enough

Face washing is hygiene, not treatment. If you have painful, cystic, or scarring breakouts, breakouts that do not improve after 8–12 weeks of a steady routine, or any spreading rash, infection, or sudden change, see a dermatologist. A professional can offer options no cleanser can. This post is general information, not medical advice, and it never prescribes treatments for skin conditions.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a day should I wash my face?

Twice a day — morning and night — is right for most people. A third wash after heavy sweating is fine. Washing more than that usually dries skin out and causes more breakouts.

Should I use hot water to wash my face?

No. Hot water strips natural oils, leaves skin tight and irritated, and can worsen redness. Lukewarm water cleans just as well without damaging the skin barrier.

Can I wash my face with water?

Water alone does not remove sunscreen, oil, and grime well. A gentle cleanser once or twice a day cleans properly; water alone is only a backup for a quick rinse.

Is double cleansing necessary?

Not for most people. One gentle cleanser is enough unless you wear heavy makeup or waterproof sunscreen, where an oil-based first cleanse can help remove it.
