Chhath Puja Grooming: Fresh Across All Four Days

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

Chhath Puja runs from Friday 13 to Monday 16 November 2026, and its defining moments happen outdoors: the evening arghya on Sunday 15 November and the dawn arghya at sunrise the next morning. Four days of fasting, family, and long hours at the ghat ask a lot of your skin and your sleep. This guide is the practical side of the festival — how to arrive at the riverbank looking rested, stay protected through hours in the sun, and still look composed in the family photographs on the final morning.

What four days of Chhath ask of your skin

Chhath is unlike a single-evening festival. The observance stretches across four days — Nahay Khay, Kharna, the evening arghya, and the morning arghya — and the people supporting the vratis spend much of it outdoors: preparing the ghat, carrying baskets, standing in or beside the water, and gathering again before dawn. Late-October and November sun in Bihar, Jharkhand, and eastern Uttar Pradesh is gentler than May sun, but hours of it still add up, and the early starts compress sleep exactly when you want to look your freshest.

Two honest limits set the plan. First, nobody transforms their skin in four days — what you can control is cleanliness, comfort, sun protection, and how rested you look. Second, the festival comes first: every step here fits around the observance, never in front of it. Treat this as a support routine for a demanding, meaningful four days.

The week before: set the base (6–12 November)

  • Settle the haircut by 8 November. A cut five to eight days before the festival has lost its raw edges and still looks sharp. The festival week itself is the wrong time for a barber queue or a new style you have not worn before.
  • Shape the beard early. If you wear a beard, have the lines cleaned and the shape set in the same barber visit. During the festival, upkeep is a rinse and a comb — nothing structural.
  • Run a steady skin base. Gentle cleanser morning and night, moisturiser after, sunscreen on any day you are outdoors. Our skincare routine for beginners lays out the full essentials in order. A week of consistency beats a shelf of new products.
  • Close the door on experiments. No new actives, scrubs, or face packs in the final week. A reaction earns you four days of redness in every photograph; a familiar routine earns you calm skin.
  • Protect your sleep early. The dawn arghya means waking well before sunrise on the final day. Shifting your sleep earlier by half an hour across the week makes that morning far easier — our sleep and skin guide explains what the last nights do for your face.

Day one and two: Nahay Khay and Kharna (13–14 November)

The first two days set the rhythm. Preparations fill the house, and the Kharna fast runs through the second day until the evening meal. Grooming stays light and functional:

  • Cleanse twice, gently. Riverbank visits, market runs, and kitchen work leave the day on your face. A gentle cleanse morning and night keeps pores clear without stripping skin that needs to stay comfortable for four days.
  • Moisturise after every wash. November air in the region turns dry, and repeated washing without moisturiser leaves skin tight and dull. Apply moisturiser to slightly damp skin so it holds water better.
  • Care through the fast. If you are observing the fast yourself, sip water in the windows where your observance allows it, and rest in the afternoon when the house schedule permits. Dry lips are the most visible sign of a long fast — keep a plain lip balm in your pocket and use it before it becomes a problem.
  • Lay out the festival clothes. Kurta washed, pressed, and ready, with a clean towel and a change for after the ghat. Preparation done on day two is stress removed from day three.

Arghya days: the ghat routine (15–16 November)

Before you leave for the evening arghya

  • Sunscreen, applied properly. The evening arghya means standing outdoors through the late afternoon. Apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher to the face, ears, neck, and the backs of the hands about twenty minutes before you step out, and reapply if you are out beyond two hours. Our sunscreen guide covers amounts and reapplication in full.
  • Matte over shine. Riverbank light is bright and direct. A light moisturiser rather than a heavy cream keeps the face from shining in photographs, and a quick blot with a clean handkerchief before pictures does the rest.
  • Neat, not styled. Hair combed, beard rinsed and patted dry, nails trimmed. Wet-look gels and heavy styling read poorly in natural light and wilt over long hours; a tidy natural finish holds all evening.

The dawn arghya (Monday 16 November)

The final morning starts in the dark. Wake, rinse the face with cool water, moisturise, and dress warmly under the kurta if the morning is cold — shivering through photographs helps nobody. Cool water and twenty minutes upright settle most sleep puffiness; our guide to looking good in photos covers the rest of the morning face honestly. After the arghya and the return home, the festival closes with family, food, and photographs — take the pictures in open shade near the ghat or at home, where the light is soft and even, rather than in direct midday sun.

What to carry to the ghat

A small kit, packed the evening before, removes most of the friction from the arghya hours:

  • A clean handkerchief or small towel — for blotting shine, drying hands, and wiping river water from the face.
  • Lip balm — the quiet fix for the most visible sign of a long fast day.
  • A spare kurta or shirt for after the morning arghya, when clothes carry river water and the family photographs begin.
  • Sunscreen, if the morning runs long or the return journey crosses bright sun.
  • Water and something light to eat once your observance allows it — energy shows on the face as plainly as tiredness does.

None of this competes with the observance. It is the same preparation the household gives to everything else across these four days — the soop, the prasad, the clothes — extended to the man carrying them.

After the festival: recover well

Four days of sun, river water, and short sleep deserve one recovery day. Cleanse gently, moisturise generously, and give skin a rest from anything active. Drink water through the day, eat properly, and take one full night of sleep — the rested look returns faster than any product can force it. If sun exposure left real redness or soreness, treat it as you would any sunburn: cool water, plain moisturiser, and a doctor if it blisters or spreads.

Frequently asked questions

When should I get my haircut before Chhath?

Five to eight days before the festival starts — between Friday 6 and Sunday 8 November 2026. A fresh cut looks raw at the edges; a week-old cut has settled while still looking sharp, and it keeps you out of the barber queue during the festival itself.

How do I protect my skin during the arghya?

Apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen to the face, ears, neck, and backs of the hands about twenty minutes before going outdoors, and reapply after two hours outside. Late-afternoon and morning sun still counts when you are standing at the ghat for long stretches.

Can I start a new skincare product during Chhath?

No. Four festival days are the wrong window for experiments — a new active or face pack can leave redness that lasts the whole observance. Stay with the cleanser and moisturiser your skin already knows, and try anything new after the festival.

How do I look less tired at the dawn arghya?

Shift your sleep earlier across the festival week, rinse with cool water on the final morning, and give puffiness twenty minutes of being upright to settle. Sleep does the real work; cool water and moisturiser finish the job.
