How to Smell Good All Day

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

Learning how to smell good all day is less about finding the best smell in a bottle and more about getting the essentials right: clean skin, fresh clothes, deodorant that works for you, fresh breath, and fragrance used lightly. Get those right and you will smell good in real life, not for the first ten minutes after you leave the house.

What does smelling good mean?

Smelling good means keeping your body, clothes, and breath fresh through hygiene. It starts with washing, clean clothes, and oral care. Fragrance is optional and should stay subtle. Overdoing fragrance pushes people away. Shower regularly, wash clothes after wear, and brush twice a day.

Smell is one of the first things people notice about you, and one of the hardest to judge in yourself — you get used to your own scent quickly. That is why a clear, repeatable routine matters more than any single product.

1. Start with clean skin

Body odour mostly comes from sweat mixing with bacteria on the skin, especially in warm, covered areas like the underarms, groin, and feet. Regular washing keeps that bacteria in check. For most people, a daily shower is enough. If you exercise, work outdoors, or live in a hot climate, a second rinse after sweating helps.

When you shower, focus on the areas where sweat collects: underarms, feet, behind the ears, and the groin area. Use a gentle soap or body wash, rinse well, and dry fully afterwards — damp skin and damp clothes encourage odour. Avoid over-washing with harsh soaps: stripping the skin can cause irritation without making you smell better. Gentle, regular washing beats aggressive scrubbing.

2. Deodorant: choose what fits your day

Deodorant and antiperspirant are not the same thing. Deodorant reduces odour. Antiperspirant also reduces sweating by temporarily blocking sweat glands. Many products combine both. Neither is right for everyone, so choose based on your skin and your day.

  • Everyday use: A plain deodorant or a mild antiperspirant is enough for most office or school days.
  • Hot days and workouts: An antiperspirant applied to clean, dry underarms gives more protection against sweat and odour.
  • Sensitive skin: If a product stings, reddens, or itches, stop using it and try a gentler, fragrance-free option.

Apply deodorant to clean, dry skin. Applying it over sweat or over yesterday's product does not work as well. If you are out for a long day, carrying a small deodorant or a pack of wipes for a quick refresh is more effective than adding more fragrance on top of sweat.

3. Fragrance essentials: less is more

Fragrance should be the finishing touch, not the foundation. It works best on clean skin and clean clothes, in small amounts. Two to three light sprays are enough for most perfumes and colognes. Good spots are pulse points where skin is warm: one spray on each wrist and one on the neck, or one on the chest under your shirt. Do not spray on broken or irritated skin.

When choosing a fragrance, think about the situation. Light, fresh scents suit daytime, school, and work. Warmer, deeper scents suit evenings. And remember: do not use fragrance to cover up odour — it usually makes things worse by mixing the two smells. You also stop noticing your own fragrance after a while, which does not mean it has gone, so resist the urge to re-spray every hour.

4. Clothes: the part most people forget

You can shower, apply deodorant, and wear fragrance, and still not smell good if your clothes are not fresh. Clothes absorb sweat, food smells, and smoke, and they hold those smells long after you have washed your body.

  • Wear a fresh shirt, underwear, and socks every day. Re-wearing a shirt that "seems fine" is one of the most common reasons people smell stale by afternoon.
  • Make sure clothes are fully dry before wearing or storing them. Clothes left damp develop a musty smell that no fragrance fixes.
  • Air out jackets, blazers, and shoes between wears, and let shoes dry fully. Rotate pairs when you can, and wash or replace smelly insoles.
  • Wash gym clothes and sports kit after every use — they are designed to absorb sweat, which means they also hold odour.
  • Wash bedding and towels weekly. They collect odour just like clothes do.

5. Fresh breath

Breath is part of how you smell, and it is easy to overlook because you cannot smell your own. Brush twice a day, floss daily, and clean your tongue gently. Stay hydrated — a dry mouth tends to smell worse, which is one reason morning breath is stronger than breath later in the day. Garlic, onion, coffee, and alcohol all affect breath for hours. If you have a meeting, a date, or a close conversation coming up, brush your teeth or chew sugar-free gum afterwards. Smoking affects breath, clothes, hair, and skin, and no mint or fragrance fully covers it.

6. The all-day routine

  • Morning: Shower, dry fully, deodorant on clean dry skin, fresh clothes, brush teeth, fragrance if you want it — two to three sprays.
  • Midday: If you have been sweating, a quick wipe of the underarms and a fresh layer of deodorant beats more fragrance. Drink water, and freshen your breath after lunch.
  • Evening: If you are going out, change into fresh clothes and reapply deodorant. One light spray of fragrance is enough.
  • Weekly: Wash bedding, towels, and shoes, and air out your wardrobe.

When to see a doctor

Most body odour improves with the habits above. But if a strong, unusual, or persistent body odour does not improve despite good hygiene, clean clothes, and deodorant — or if it appears suddenly alongside other symptoms — speak to a doctor. This guide does not diagnose or treat any condition. Getting checked is sensible, not embarrassing.

Frequently asked questions

How can I smell good all day?

Start with the essentials: shower regularly, use deodorant, wear clean clothes, and keep your breath fresh. Add fragrance lightly if you like it. Freshness lasts longer when the essentials are covered than when fragrance is used to cover up odour.

Should I use deodorant or antiperspirant?

Deodorant reduces odour, while antiperspirant also reduces sweating. Many people use an antiperspirant on warm days or during exercise and a plain deodorant otherwise. Choose what suits your skin and your day, and stop using a product that irritates your skin.

How much perfume or cologne should I use?

Less than you think. Two to three light sprays on pulse points — for example, one on each wrist and one on the neck — is enough for most fragrances. The goal is that people notice it when they are close to you, not from across the room.

Why do I still smell after showering?

Common causes are clothes or shoes that are not fully clean or dry, not washing areas where sweat collects, and strong-smelling food or smoke on clothes. If a strong or unusual body odour persists despite good hygiene, speak to a doctor.
