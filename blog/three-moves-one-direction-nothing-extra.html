What this poster is really saying

"Three moves. One direction. Nothing extra." sounds like discipline talk, and at its root is something kinder: make the right thing the easy thing. Lay out the morning the night before, keep the habit small enough to do on a bad day, and track it plainly. Discipline is mostly design wearing a stern face.

Review weekly, not daily. Daily judgement makes normal variance feel like failure; weekly review shows the true pattern. Ask one question: did the habit happen most days? If yes, keep going. If no, shrink it further. The standard bends; it does not break. That is how "Three moves. One direction. Nothing extra." stops being a poster and starts being a Tuesday.

Take it with you

  • Attach new habits to ones that already happen.
  • Track with one mark a day; the streak becomes its own reason.
  • Design the environment: remove decisions in advance.

Frequently asked questions

What does "Three moves. One direction. Nothing extra." mean in practice?

It is a poster line with a practical core. "Three moves. One direction. Nothing extra." is the whole poster in one breath: keep the standard small, daily, and honest — the rest follows. The point is not the poster on a wall — it is one small standard, kept most days, inside a routine you can sustain for a full 90-day cycle.

Why do my routines keep falling apart after two weeks?

Usually one of three things: the habit was too big, it had no fixed trigger in the day, or it was never tracked. Fix it by shrinking the habit, anchoring it to something that already happens, and marking one X per day. Two weeks is exactly when design flaws show — treat the failure as feedback, not as character.

Is a morning routine actually worth it?

Yes, if it is short and honest. A good morning routine is not a performance — it is five to twenty minutes that decide the tone of the next few hours: light, water, movement, one clear plan. Skip the theatre; keep the sequence. On the worst mornings, run the two-minute version and count it as kept.
