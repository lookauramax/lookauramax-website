What this poster is really saying

"Breathe in. Begin." sounds like a compliment waiting to happen, but it points at something practical: people who look after their teeth smile more freely, and freer smiles read as warmer. The routine is unglamorous — brush, floss, check-ups — and the payoff is entirely social.

If you hide your smile in photos, start there: take a few relaxed photos at home until smiling for the lens stops feeling performed. A smile is a practice, not a feature — the more freely it appears, the more natural it reads. That is how "Breathe in. Begin." stops being a poster and starts being a Tuesday.

Take it with you

  • Keep the six-monthly dental check-up — prevention beats repair.
  • Drink water through the day; dry mouth dulls a smile.
  • Ask a dentist before whitening products — protect the enamel.

Frequently asked questions

What does "Breathe in. Begin." mean in practice?

It is a poster line with a practical core. "Breathe in. Begin." is the whole poster in one breath: keep the standard small, daily, and honest — the rest follows. The point is not the poster on a wall — it is one small standard, kept most days, inside a routine you can sustain for a full 90-day cycle.

Does flossing really matter?

Yes — brushing cleans the faces of teeth, and floss cleans between them, where gum problems start. Bleeding gums at first are usually a sign to keep going gently, not to stop. Once a day is enough; the habit matters more than the technique being perfect.

Why do I look awkward when I smile in photos?

Most people tense their whole face for the camera. Relax the jaw, think of something genuinely amusing, and let the eyes smile too — a real smile uses the muscles around the eyes. Practice at home until it stops feeling performed.
