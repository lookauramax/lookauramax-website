How to Trim a Mustache

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 7 Nov 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

A good mustache trim is mostly restraint. Small cuts, dry hair, and knowing when to stop. The whole job is this: comb it down, clean the line above your lip, tidy the top edge under your nose, and leave the rest alone. Most bad trims come from the same two mistakes — cutting while the hair is wet, and cutting too much at once.

Pick your tool first

Scissors or trimmer is the first choice, and it depends on the mustache you are keeping. Neither is better in general — they are better for different jobs.

Small scissors are the tool for control. They suit longer styles — a handlebar, a walrus, anything where the ends need their length — and for coarse hairs that need one snip at a time. Use scissors made for facial hair: small, sharp, straight-edged. Household scissors are too big for the space above your lip, and too close to your mouth to risk. A fine-tooth mustache comb goes with them. You comb first, then cut what sticks out.

A trimmer is the tool for speed and even length. It suits shorter, structured styles like a chevron or a beardstache, where you want one controlled length across the whole mustache. Use one with a guard, and check what the guard actually cuts — guard numbers are not the same on every trimmer, so read the marked length instead of trusting the number. Gillette's guidance, from pro barber Jason Schneidman of the Gillette Barber Council, is to start with the longest comb and work down one setting at a time. You can always trim more. You cannot put hair back.

If you own both, use both. The trimmer sets the length, the scissors finish the lip line and the strays. That is how most barbers work.

Wait until there is something to trim

A mustache needs about two centimetres of length before it can be properly trimmed and shaped, and for most men that takes two to three weeks of growth. Before that, there is nothing to shape — only the lip line.

The most common way to ruin a young mustache is to start too early. In the first two weeks it looks patchy and uneven, and the temptation is to even it out by cutting. Cutting it back only resets the clock. Our beard-growth guide makes the same point about beards: the early weeks look worse than the result. If your mustache is under three weeks old, trim only the hairs that hang over your lip. Leave the body alone.

The Movember guide on this site says the same thing for a different reason: during a growth month, length is what makes later styling possible, so you clean the lip line and let the rest grow. The rule holds all year round.

The steps

Start with a clean, completely dry mustache. Wash it first if it needs it, then dry it fully. Wet hair lies — it hangs longer than it really is, so a trim that looks right in the shower is too short by the time it dries.

Work through the steps in this order. Choose the shape first, comb, trim a little, check. That order is the whole method.

  1. Comb it down. Run a fine-tooth mustache comb straight down toward your lip, then slightly out toward the edges. This shows the true length and shape, and it pulls the strays into view. Comb outward instead if you are keeping length for curled ends.
  2. Clean the lip line. This is the part that matters most. With your face relaxed — mouth closed, no smile, no frown — cut only the hairs that hang past your upper lip, working from the centre outward in small snips. The lip line is the whole job for most men.
  3. Tidy the top edge. Start directly under the septum, the middle strip between your nostrils, and work down along the top line of the mustache on each side. Take a little at a time. This edge frames the nose, so a heavy hand here shows immediately.
  4. Take the bulk down, if it needs it. Only now, with the edges set, decide whether the body of the mustache is too long. Comb it down again and snip the hairs that stick out beyond the shape you want, one or two millimetres at a time. For a handlebar or walrus, skip this step — the length is the style.
  5. Taper the ends. Comb the outer hairs toward the edges of your face and snip them to a gradual taper, so the mustache does not end in a blunt block. With a trimmer, use a shorter guard at the edges and work from the outside in.
  6. Check, then stop. Step back and look at both sides from the front and from a slight angle. If one side looks shorter, stop — the fix is never to cut the other side to match. Most lopsided mustaches are made in the second round of fixing the first.

With scissors

Scissors are the slower method, and that is their advantage. Comb the mustache down. Start at the centre of your upper lip and work outward, snipping only the hairs that cross the lip line, keeping the scissors flat to the lip. Then move to the body: work outward from the centre, a little on each side, so the two halves stay balanced. Snip individual strays that sit outside the shape you want.

For a longer style, the scissors are the only tool that works. A trimmer guard takes the whole mustache to one length, and a handlebar needs its ends long. Keep the length, clean the line, and let the ends do what they do.

After the trim, a drop of beard oil worked through the mustache keeps it soft and makes the shape easier to see. Our beard-oil guide covers the amount — one drop, warmed between your fingertips, combed down and out from the centre, kept off the lip line.

With a trimmer

Start with the longest guard you think might be too long, and check its marked cutting length before you switch the trimmer on. Glide it across the mustache following the shape — right under the nose, angle it slightly outward. Then lower the guard by one setting and repeat, only if the length still needs it.

Pop the guard off for the lip line. Use the corner of the blade, or the detail blade if your trimmer has one, and trace your natural lip shape with light movements. You are cleaning up the hairs that hang over, not carving a sharp line. Check the outline with your mouth relaxed — a smile pulls the skin up and changes where the line sits.

Two warnings. Never run an unguarded trimmer across the whole mustache — it takes off more than you meant to, in one pass, with no way back. And trim against the grain for the bulk, but keep the detail work slow and light. A trimmer is fast, and fast is how a mustache gets shorter on one side.

How often to trim

Every three to five days keeps a mustache sharp. That is the usual rhythm: the lip line is the first part to grow into the mouth, so check it every few days and clean it before it starts catching food. Once a week is enough if you are growing it out — the bulk needs time, and the lip line is the only part that needs regular attention.

Mustache hair grows at the same rough pace as the rest of your facial hair, about 0.3 to 0.5 millimetres a day. A week without a trim is two to three millimetres of extra length, and most of that shows at the lip. If you shave the rest of your face, trim the mustache in the same session. Our shaving guide covers the cadence for the rest of the face.

Trim in the morning, in good light, when you are not in a rush. A mustache trim takes five minutes when it goes well and a month to grow back when it does not.

Five mistakes that ruin a mustache

Trimming it wet. Wet hair hangs longer than dry hair, and every ruined trim starts here. Dry it fully first.

One big cut. Trying to take the whole mustache to length in a single pass — with scissors or a trimmer — is how one side ends up shorter. Small cuts, check after every few, and stop early.

Guard roulette. Picking a guard by its number without checking the marked length. A "3" on one trimmer is not a "3" on another. Start long — you can always go shorter.

Chasing symmetry by cutting shorter. One side looks a millimetre longer, so you cut it, and now it looks shorter, so you cut the other side, and now both are too short. Nobody sees the difference you see in the mirror. Stop after the first pass.

Trimming the bulk for a long style. A handlebar or walrus needs its ends. Running a guard over the whole thing turns it into a chevron. If you want the length, protect it — scissors only, lip line only.

When it will not sit right

Some mustaches fight back, and the cause is usually not the trim. A mustache that curls up at the ends is doing what facial hair does — the hair is coarse and the growth points outward. Our curly-beard guide explains the same thing for beards: the curl comes from the shape of the follicle, and no amount of trimming straightens it. Comb it down, keep the ends if you like the curl, or trim them short if you do not.

A gap in the middle, or a mustache that does not connect to the beard, is usually genetics — the same reason some men grow a full beard and some do not. A shorter, tidier mustache looks deliberate even with modest density, and clean edges do more for it than extra length. The Movember guide says the same for patchy growth: keep it shorter, keep the edges clean, and let the full month pass before deciding it does not suit you.

If the skin under the mustache itches, the problem is the skin, not the hair. Wash the mustache, moisturise the skin beneath it, and leave it alone for a few days. Our neckline guide covers the same clean-edge thinking for the beard below the jaw — the edge is the style, and the bulk can wait.

And if the mustache keeps catching food and drink no matter how often you trim it, the lip line is not the problem — the length above it is. Take the bulk down by a guard setting. A shorter mustache that stays out of the way beats a long one you are always wiping.

Frequently asked questions

How do you trim a mustache with scissors?

Comb it down while it is fully dry, hold small sharp scissors flat to the lip, and snip only the hairs that hang past the lip line, working from the centre outward, one or two millimetres at a time. Scissors are the tool for longer styles and detail work — a handlebar keeps its ends only if you never run a guard over them.

How do you trim a mustache with a trimmer?

Start with the longest guard, check its marked cutting length first, and work it across the mustache following the shape. Drop one setting at a time only if the length still needs it. Pop the guard off only for the lip line and the edges — never run an unguarded trimmer across the whole mustache.

How do you trim a mustache above the lip?

Comb it straight down, let your face relax with your mouth closed, and cut only the hairs that hang past the lip line, parallel to the lip. That line is the whole job for most men — leave the body of the mustache alone unless it is clearly too long.

How often should you trim a mustache?

Every three to five days keeps it sharp; once a week is enough if you are growing it out. The lip line is the part to check most often, because it is the first to grow into the mouth. Trim in the morning, in good light, when you are not in a rush — five minutes done properly beats a month of growing a mistake back.
