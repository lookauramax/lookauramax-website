How to Take a Good Scan Photo: Lighting, Angle, Framing

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

A scan is only as fair as the photo behind it. Good light, eye-level camera, neutral expression, plain background — that is the whole recipe, and it matters more than any setting in the app.

What is a good scan photo?

A good scan photo is a clear, front-facing photo in good light. It shows your face centred, with a neutral expression and no filters. It lets AuraScan measure fairly. A bad photo produces no score, not a low score from AuraScan.

Most bad scan results are not bad skin or bad features. They are bad photos. A dark bathroom, a low angle, a harsh overhead light, a grin held too hard — each one changes what the camera sees, and therefore what any analysis can fairly say. The good news is that a fair photo setup takes two minutes and no equipment. This guide covers it step by step, plus the mistakes that make scans unfair and how to keep progress photos comparable across 90 days.

The 60-second setup

  1. Find a window. Stand facing a large window in daylight. The light should fall evenly on your face. Morning or late-afternoon light is ideal; harsh midday sun through the window is too contrasty.
  2. Turn off the overhead light. Mixed lighting (window plus ceiling bulb) creates uneven colour and shadows. One light source — the window — is fairer.
  3. Phone at eye level. Hold the phone (or set it on a shelf) at eye level, about an arm's length away, front-facing. The face should be centred in the frame with the full head and a little shoulder visible.
  4. Plain background. A plain wall behind you. A busy background distracts the eye and the measurement.
  5. Neutral expression. Lips closed, jaw unclenched, eyes relaxed, looking at the lens. Not smiling hard, not frowning, not raising brows.
  6. Hold steady. Brace your elbows or prop the phone. Blur is the most common reason scans come back low-confidence.

Lighting: the details that matter

  • Face the window, not the sun. Direct sun creates hard shadows and squinting. Bright, indirect daylight is the goal — a cloudy day is actually excellent for scan photos.
  • No backlight. If the window is behind you, your face goes dark and the camera compensates badly. You face the light; the light faces you.
  • No flash. Phone flash creates harsh highlights, red-eye, and flattened texture. Natural light only.
  • No coloured lights. Warm yellow lamps, neon signs, and coloured LEDs tint the skin and make colour-based signals meaningless. Daylight or neutral white only.
  • Same light every time. For progress photos, use the same window, same time of day, same setup. Comparing a sunny Day 0 to a cloudy Day 90 is not a fair comparison.

Angle and framing

  • Eye level, front-facing. The camera should be level with your eyes, pointing straight at your face. This is the only angle that is fair for comparison.
  • Avoid low angles. Shooting from below makes the jaw and chin look heavier and the nostrils more prominent. It is the least flattering and least fair angle.
  • Avoid steep high angles. Shooting from far above shrinks the jaw and enlarges the forehead. A very slight elevation (a few degrees) is fine; a steep selfie angle is not.
  • Head straight, not tilted. Keep the head level — no strong tilt, no chin tucked hard down, no chin jutting forward. Chin level, shoulders back and down, as in our posture guide.
  • Full head in frame. Top of hair to below the chin, plus a little shoulder. A cropped forehead or chin makes the reading unfair.
  • Distance: one arm's length. Too close distorts the face (wide-angle distortion); too far loses detail. Arm's length with the rear camera is the practical sweet spot.

Expression, grooming, and timing

  • Expression: relaxed and neutral. A forced grin tightens the eyes and changes the mouth shape. A frown does the reverse. Neutral is the only repeatable expression.
  • Fresh face: take the scan before heavy makeup, filters, or beauty mode. Beauty mode smooths texture — it makes the scan meaningless. Turn it off.
  • Consistent grooming: for progress comparison, take Aura Check-in scans at a similar grooming state (for example, after your usual trim, not the day before). Grooming changes are real progress, but they should not be confused with skin changes.
  • Same time of day: morning puffiness is real. Take Aura Check-in photos at the same time of day — ideally mid-morning, after you have been up for an hour or two.

Common mistakes

  • Bathroom mirror selfie. Overhead light plus a mirror plus a phone blocking part of the face. Almost always unfair.
  • Car selfie. Mixed light, sunglasses marks, seatbelt shadows, weird angles. Skip it.
  • Night photo with flash. Harsh, uneven, unrepresentative.
  • Filtered or beauty-mode photo. Smoothed skin, reshaped features. The scan cannot be honest about a photo that is not honest.
  • Different setup each time. The most common progress-tracking mistake. Change the setup and you cannot tell whether the face changed or the light did.
  • Sunglasses, hats, heavy shadows. Anything covering the face or casting shadows across it makes the reading unfair.

Why AuraScan sometimes says "no score"

If a scan comes back low-confidence with no score, that is the system working correctly. It means the photo was too dark, too blurry, or too poorly framed to score fairly — and inventing a number would be worse than saying nothing. Progress photos across 90 days

  1. Pick one spot — one window, one wall, one phone position. Mark it if you need to.
  2. Take Aura Check-ins at Day 0, 30, 60, and 90, same time of day, same setup, same expression.
  3. Store them in the same place and compare side by side. Do not compare a Day 0 bathroom selfie to a Day 90 window photo.
  4. Judge trends, not single photos. One photo can be flattered by light; four consistent photos tell the truth.

A fair photo is a kindness to yourself: it means your scores track your actual routine, not your lighting luck. Two minutes of setup, same every time — that is the whole secret.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best lighting for a scan photo?

Soft, even daylight facing a window. Face the window, not the sun, and avoid overhead light, backlight, and flash.

What angle should a scan photo use?

Camera at eye level, front-facing, with the face centred. Avoid low angles and steep high angles, which distort the jaw and neck.

Should I smile in a scan photo?

Use a relaxed, neutral expression with lips closed and jaw unclenched. A forced grin or a frown changes the reading.

Why did my scan say low confidence?

The photo was too dark, blurry, or poorly framed to score fairly. Retake it with better light, a steady hand, and the face centred — do not expect a number from a bad photo.
