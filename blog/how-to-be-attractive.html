How to Be More Attractive (the Honest Way)

By Lookauramax Team · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 · General information, not medical advice.

If you want to learn how to be attractive, start by ignoring anyone who sells you a single trick, a single product, or a single number. Attractiveness is the combined effect of grooming, posture, health, style, behaviour, and how you make other people feel — and almost all of it is built from small, repeatable habits.

What is attractiveness?

Attractiveness is how appealing someone finds you, shaped by grooming, posture, health, kindness, and confidence. It is subjective and partly out of your control. The honest part is what you can change: grooming, posture, health, and how you treat people.

That is good news. You do not need to become a different person or chase an impossible standard. Take care of the parts of your appearance and behaviour that are genuinely within your control, and let the results accumulate over weeks and months.

What attractiveness actually is

When people say someone is attractive, they are rarely judging one feature in isolation. They are responding to a whole picture: clean appearance, relaxed posture, healthy energy, clothes that fit, a natural expression, and how that person treats others. No single feature decides it, and no app or product can honestly reduce a person to an attractiveness number. How you look matters, because first impressions are real. But how you behave matters just as much. Someone who is well-groomed but rude or dishonest is hard to be around. Someone who is kind, curious, and comfortable in their own skin is easy to be around.

1. Grooming: the highest-return habit

Grooming is the fastest, lowest-cost, most reliable way to look better, because it is fully within your control and the results are visible the same day:

  • Shower regularly, wash your face twice a day with a gentle cleanser, and moisturise.
  • Keep nails short and clean, brush and floss daily, and keep facial hair intentional — clean-shaven or a neatly trimmed beard.
  • Get a haircut that suits your hair type, and maintain it every four to six weeks.
  • Wear clean clothes that fit. Fit matters more than brand or price: a well-fitting plain shirt beats an expensive shirt that is too tight or too baggy.

2. Posture: stand like you are comfortable

Posture changes how you look in seconds and how you feel over time. Standing tall with relaxed shoulders, a level chin, and your weight evenly distributed makes you look more confident and approachable. Slouching does the opposite — it can make you look tired or closed off, even when you are not.

Good posture is not about forcing your chest out. Think of it as stacking: ears over shoulders, shoulders over hips, hips over ankles. Unclench your jaw, drop your shoulders, and let your arms hang naturally. Our posture guide covers clear daily exercises that help without any equipment.

3. Health: sleep, movement, and food

Health shows on your face and in your energy. Aim for seven to nine hours of sleep most nights, keep a roughly regular schedule, and avoid bright screens in the last half hour before bed.

Move regularly in a way you will actually keep doing: walking, strength training two or three times a week, or any sport you enjoy. Eat a generally balanced diet — enough protein, plenty of vegetables and fruit, enough water, and less ultra-processed food and alcohol. Avoid crash diets; they are hard to sustain and often leave you feeling worse.

4. Style: fit, simplicity, and occasion

Style is not about following every trend. It is about wearing clothes that fit your body, suit the occasion, and feel like you. A small wardrobe of well-fitting essentials — clean shoes, dark jeans or trousers, a few good shirts, a jacket that fits — will carry most people further than a closet full of statement pieces.

5. Kindness and social habits

This is the part most "how to be attractive" content skips, and it is the part that matters most in real relationships. People are drawn to those who make them feel seen, respected, and at ease: listening properly, remembering what people tell you, keeping your promises, and treating everyone with the same core respect. It also means handling disagreement well — staying calm, admitting mistakes, and apologising sincerely.

6. Confidence: built, not performed

Real confidence is not a pose. It is the quiet result of keeping small promises to yourself: showing up, following through, and doing hard things even when they are uncomfortable. Collect evidence — finish the workout, speak up once in the meeting, start the conversation. Avoid comparing yourself to edited photos and highlight reels, and do not wait to feel confident before acting. Confidence usually follows action, not the other way round.

What to ignore

Ignore anything that promises a certain outcome, a fast transformation, or a secret that "nobody wants you to know." Ignore products that claim to reshape your jaw, change your bone structure, or lighten your skin — those claims are not honest, and some of the products behind them are unsafe. Ignore anyone who rates people with a number.

A clear 7-day starting plan

  • Day 1: Grooming essentials — haircut booked, nails trimmed, clothes washed.
  • Day 2: Fix your sleep: regular bedtime and a wind-down routine.
  • Day 3: Move for 30 minutes; practise posture resets through the day.
  • Day 4: Sort one outfit that fits well and feels like you.
  • Day 5: Start a clear skincare routine, morning and evening.
  • Day 6: Have one genuine conversation — listen more than you speak.
  • Day 7: Review the week, keep what worked, and repeat.

Frequently asked questions

How can I be more attractive?

Focus on what you can control: consistent grooming, good posture, enough sleep, regular movement, clothes that fit, and treating people well. Attractiveness is a mix of appearance, health, behaviour, and how you make others feel — not one feature or one number.

Does confidence make someone more attractive?

Yes, in the sense that calm, self-assured behaviour is easier to be around. Confidence here means being comfortable with yourself, listening well, and not needing constant approval — not loudness, arrogance, or pretending to be someone else.

Can you change how attractive you are?

You can improve the parts that are within your control — grooming, fitness, posture, style, sleep, and social habits — and those changes are often noticeable within weeks. You cannot change your bone structure or genetics, and no product or exercise honestly promises to.

What makes someone unattractive?

Nothing about a person makes them worthless or unworthy of respect. Habits that tend to push people away are poor hygiene, constant negativity, disrespect, dishonesty, and treating others as a means to an end. Those are behaviours, and behaviours can change.
