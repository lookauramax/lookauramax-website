Winter skin is not a mystery: cold air holds less moisture, indoor heating dries the air further, and hot showers strip the oils that protect you. Change five things in your routine and most winter tightness, flaking, and rough shaves go with them.

What happens to your skin in winter?

In winter, the air outside carries very little moisture, and heated indoor air is drier still. Your skin loses water to that dry air faster than it can replace it, and the barrier — the thin layer of oils and lipids that slows water loss — weakens. The result: skin that feels tight after washing, flakes at the edges of the nose and brows, itches under clothing, and makes shaving feel rougher. Fine lines also look more visible on dehydrated skin, which is why faces often look more tired in January than they did in October.

The encouraging part: winter dryness is mostly an environment problem, not a skin-type sentence. Men with oily summers get tight cheeks in winter; men with balanced skin get flaky foreheads. The routine has to change with the season, and five changes cover nearly all of it.

The five changes that fix winter skin

1. Move to a gentler cleanser

The foaming or oil-stripping cleanser that felt fine in August is often the main cause of winter tightness. Switch to a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser — cream or low-foam — and if your skin runs dry, cleanse with product once a day in the evening and rinse with lukewarm water in the morning. If your face feels squeaky after washing, the cleanser is too strong for the season. Our double cleansing guide explains when a second cleanse helps and when it only strips.

2. Step up to a richer moisturiser, applied on damp skin

A summer lotion may not hold through a winter day. Look for a cream with ceramides, glycerin, or hyaluronic acid on the label — ingredients that support the barrier and draw water into the skin. Apply within a few minutes of washing, while skin is still slightly damp: moisturiser seals in the water already on your skin, which is most of its effect. Tight by midday means step up the night cream first; the night is when skin does its repair work.

3. Keep the sunscreen

Winter sun is weaker in the rays that burn, but UVA — the kind linked to ageing and uneven tone — passes through cloud and glass in every season. Broad-spectrum SPF 30 every morning remains the highest-value habit in skincare, winter included. The full reasoning is in our sunscreen guide.

4. Turn the shower down

Long, hot showers feel earned in cold weather and are a leading cause of winter dryness: hot water dissolves the barrier oils efficiently, and the longer you stand in it, the more you lose. Shorter, lukewarm showers — especially for the face — make a measurable difference within a week. Moisturise your body within a few minutes of stepping out, while skin is damp: elbows, knees, shins, and hands first, because they dry fastest.

5. Cover the two forgotten places: lips and hands

Lips have no oil glands of their own and take wind directly; a plain lip balm, reapplied through the day and before bed, prevents the cracking cycle. Hands are washed constantly and exposed to cold air, so they dry and crack first for many men — hand cream after washing, and before sleep, keeps them comfortable. These are small habits with an outsized effect on how groomed you look in winter.

The winter routine, morning and night

Morning

  1. Rinse or gentle cleanse. Lukewarm water; the gentle cleanser only if your skin needs it.
  2. Moisturiser. The richer cream, on slightly damp skin, including the neck and ears.
  3. Sunscreen. Broad-spectrum SPF 30, every morning, all winter.
  4. Lip balm. Before you leave the house.

Night

  1. Gentle cleanser. Wash off sunscreen, sweat, and the day — one thorough, kind cleanse.
  2. Moisturiser, slightly richer. If cheeks or the brow area still feel tight, this is the layer to strengthen.
  3. Lip balm and hand cream. The night shift, while skin repairs.

If you use an active such as a retinoid or a strong exfoliant and winter makes your skin sting or peel, reduce frequency rather than pushing through — irritated winter skin heals slowly, and a calm barrier beats an ambitious routine. Shaving on dry winter skin

Winter is when shaving irritation peaks, because the blade crosses drier, less flexible skin. Shave after a warm shower, when hair is softest; let a cushioning shave gel sit for half a minute before the first pass; shave with the grain and rinse the blade often; then rinse cool, pat dry, and moisturise. Fewer passes at lower pressure beat one aggressive pass in every season, and doubly so in this one. A blade past its best drags — replace on schedule rather than pressing harder.

Fix the indoor air, too

If your home or office heating runs all day, the air around you may be the driest your skin meets. A humidifier in the bedroom is the one device that genuinely helps winter skin, because you spend a third of your life there and the night is when skin recovers. Drink water steadily through the day — not because water hydrates skin directly in the way marketing suggests, but because being dehydrated overall shows in your face quickly. And if your skin is tightest on waking, check whether the bedroom is overheated through the night; cooler air with adequate bedding is kinder.

When dry skin needs a doctor

Ordinary winter dryness improves within one to two weeks of the changes above. See a dermatologist if skin cracks or bleeds, if itching keeps you awake, if you develop spreading redness or a rash, or if dry patches persist despite a kind routine — conditions such as eczema need proper treatment, and a professional will settle in one visit what months of product changes cannot.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my skin get so dry in winter?

Cold air holds less moisture, and indoor heating dries the air further, so your skin loses water faster than it can replace it. Hot showers strip away the natural oils that slow that loss down, which is why skin feels tightest right after washing.

Should men still wear sunscreen in winter?

Yes. The burning rays fall in winter, but UVA — the kind linked to ageing and uneven tone — passes through clouds and glass all year. A broad-spectrum SPF 30 each morning remains the highest-value skincare habit in every season.

What moisturiser ingredients help dry winter skin?

Look for ceramides, glycerin, and hyaluronic acid on the label. Creams are richer than lotions and usually suit winter better. Apply within a few minutes of washing, while skin is still slightly damp, to lock water in.

How do I stop flaky skin and beard dandruff in winter?

Moisturise the skin under the beard, not only the hair: work moisturiser or a few drops of beard oil down to the skin, brush flakes out gently, and avoid very hot water on your face. Persistent scaling or redness deserves a dermatologist visit.
